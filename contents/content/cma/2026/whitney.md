Well this was a really special trip. A few months ago, Marie lucked into a 2 night wilderness
permit in the Whitney Zone. She invited us folks on the Mountain Pals chat to join if we
could, and I couldn't say no. By combining the trip down to California with another very
special trip to visit old friends in LA, I'd have the best of all possible worlds!

Marie and Emilee would be coming down straight from a run at Mount Shasta. In the end, they got
very close to the summit, but attempting the whole thing in a day ended up being logistically
problematic, and they (showing good character) turned around a few hundred feet from the 
top in ridiculous wind. We all met at the Whitney Portal Campground, enjoyed a good dinner,
and went to sleep, keen to hike early to avoid heat down at this low elevation of 8,000 feet!

Sean H was also with us. In the morning, we started up, me and Emilee in the lead until reaching
a creek to fill water near Lone Pine Lake. We continued up to Mirror Lake, where we hung out
for a full six hours in order to avoid hanging out up high in broiling sun above the trees.

During this time, I shared a logic problem I'd learned about, where blue and brown-eyed people
live on an island. The blue-eyed people are ordered to leave, however they aren't doing it
because no one is allowed to speak or signal in any way about eye color. So you don't know if
you have blue eyes -- how can you leave? But a visitor comes one day and says "I have seen
someone with blue eyes."

That unleashed the whirlwind. But the whirlwind takes a curious form, which I must express
in a somewhat stilted way. Here is what happens:

* If there are N blue-eyed people on the island, then on day N (a boat leaves every morning),
  all N blue-eyed people get on the boat and leave.

The logic puzzle solution is to explain why this happens.

We had a lot of fun with it! If fun can include frustration, that is. But mountain climbing
includes frustration, yes?

As Sean pointed out later, the salient point of the exercise is in recognizing how public
knowledge can deeply affect private, individual reasoning. Anyway, if you search on the web
for the problem you'll find it. Give it a shot...accept a hint if need be, but don't just
read the answer! That would be like taking a lift to the top of the mountain, and around here,
that *simply isn't done*.

After our long break, we hiked up to a shady spot at the terminus of the great granite wall
above Mirror Lake, then finished the slog to Trail Camp. We'd considered camping at
Consultation Lake, but since it didn't have trees, and it was at a lower elevation than
Trail Camp, we couldn't muster sufficient enthusiasm -- better to camp as high as possible
to make summit day shorter.

We loved our "freeze-dried" dinners. Those things are really good. I was enjoying my new stove
which performed really well. Marie had an interesting meal where the stove was contained in
the food itself, as a kind of chemical reaction, so she didn't need the novelty of an external
butane cannister and a Pocket Rocket.

We slept, with Mount Muir and other peaks of the crest above us. We got up at 4:30 am,
and were pretty much off by 5:00. I went a bit ahead, and found to my delight that the trips
to high elevation over the previous week had *finally* paid off! I was able to move quickly,
and without that exhausting feeling that had dogged me for White Mountain and Bear Creek Spire.
Really enjoying the switchbacks along a kind of balcony trail in a steep granite slope,
I observed the dawn a little bit below the Trail Crest. I even ran occasionally above that.
I was on top after 2 hours, 20 minutes of hiking, amid a crowd of all and sundry.

Before long, Emilee was there, then Sean and Marie. Marie fought through some nausea and
headache, which was frustrating because she's always been great at altitude. Big congratulations
for pushing through! We took a bunch of pictures and reluctantly made our way down.

I found the "goat trail" Marie had identified to head up to Mount Muir, then went up it
with Sean and Emilee. We had a picture with a little blue line on it (from Summitpost.org) showing
the easiest scrambling way. We followed this exactly, and soon sat rather nervously on the
summit. It felt very exposed! As usual, we felt much better once we'd reversed the
interesting moves and stood by our packs at the base of the summit block.

We took off for camp, me finding a JMT hiker and quizzing him about many particulars. He was fast,
so the two of us ran down a bit ahead. Marie was counting switchbacks (I think 97?).

At camp, the tent was awfully hot and uncomfortable. I packed up and stomped down to Mirror
Lake. The rest of the crew found me there and bade me continue with them to Outpost Camp.
We went there, slightly unhappy with the campsites...either in the sun or right by the trail.
I was so tired, I lay in my tent and spent an hour alternately napping and blowing up my
sleeping pad, which acquired a layer of drool because I kept falling asleep like a baby
at the breast!

Haha...

Anyway, eventually the sun went away and we had a good dinner. Poor Emilee had hikers stomping
by her tent all night, once even tripping on a guy wire. I couldn't get back to sleep after
stepping out for a leak after midnight, so eventually I decided to pack up and hike down.
I was keen on some "Nutter Butter" cookies in the bear box by my car, and I knew I could
sleep in the back, too.

So I enjoyed a wonderful night hike. California is so great for that. The temperature is pleasant,
and the night is invigorating. Large parties of hikers kept passing me, confused by my
odd presence. "Are you just now coming down?" they would ask. I'd just say yes, without explaining
further. Better to let them go, and let me go back to my communion with the night. Deer
were nestling in the forest, and would look at me with steady twin eyes. I would murmur a
good evening to them, not keen to startle them.

I was sleeping by 3:30 am, and set the alarm for 7:00 when I thought the store would open for
tea and strumpets! Alas, it wouldn't open until 9. So reading and meditation were my lot, sitting
by the store. They opened and I patiently waited about 7 minutes for the team to arrive. When I
didn't see them, I ordered a huge breakfast. A few minutes later, they arrived. They'd
visited Lone Pine Lake on the way down, an admirable side journey that yielded beautiful
pictures. Over a delicious breakfast we talked about assisted suicide, about dementia and
similar topics. My goodness...for basically happy people (as we certainly are), we can
venture into deep and dark forests as we converse!

You know, I do a lot of solitary trips, and not many with a larger group. I had so much fun,
and I think we did so well in our management of energy, of sun and shade, of pacing and
appropriate carefulness. The "brain" we made from the four of us was the best brain. A much
better one than this tired old "Kopf." A big thanks to Sean and Emilee and Marie, who kicked
it all off which an invitation.
