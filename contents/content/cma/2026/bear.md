Arjun and I met on a Facebook group for people looking
for partners in the Sierras. We quickly established
rapport and an objective: Bear Creek Spire by the North
Arete. I'd done this route years ago with Aidan, and
remembered it as a great day trip. Arjun and I shared
a dinner and got our packs ready the night before.

We were walking from the trailhead a bit after 5 am,
admiring the stars and the softly glowing lakes.
As the sky lightened, we followed a rough trail
above Gem Lake, then traversed a hillside of
manzanita bushes and talus fields. Eventually we
made it to Dade Lake, then found ourselves scrambling
steep talus above it to get below the mountain.
Another party, Dwayne and Scott were leaving their
packs at the point where the route down from the
ridge meets the point to scramble up to our route's
base. We left our packs here, too and followed them.

At the base, we started up to the right of Scott and
Dwayne. All of us were pitching it out to reach the
base of the first pitch according to guidebooks.

Here is a detailed description of the pitches as we
did them:

1. Up from the buttress toe in cracks to a ledge (25 m, 5.2). We
   made this very short pitch because the other party
   (Dwayne and Scott) hadn't had enough rope to reach
   the top of pitch 1 proper from our common stance.
   By moving the belay to this ledge, we could avoid
   building a belay in the middle of the first pitch.
2. Joining the normal route to the left in cracks, then
   departing it right on a striking flake up and further
   right to a good ledge. Some crumbly, lichen-covered
   rock in the second half (50 m, 5.7). I didn't like
   this variation much, but I can't blame Arjun for
   being inspired by the striking flake going up and right.
   It looked pretty cool, even if it wasn't!
3. Around a corner to the left to join the standard second
   pitch. Great climbing in cracks and flakes to a good
   ledge on the crest (45 m, 5.7).
4. 5.4 step-around move to the right on the crest, then
   3rd class to a ledge. Short pitch, in order to continue
   with simul-climbing into the next pitch (5.4, 25 m).
5. The ridge steepens to sustained 4th class in cracks and
   faces, simul-climbed to a ledge thought to be below
   the 5.8 crux (60 m, 4th).
6. This was the wrong location, so we moved the belay 5 meters
   to the left (5 m, 5.0).
7. The crux pitch, a mix of crack and off-width climbing in
   giant blocks, horns and flakes. Then a long 5.6 chimney
   (50 m, 5.8).
8. Passing through a tunnel to the left side of the crest, then
   traversing easy ledges. Don't climb to the crest too early
   here (I made this error, then downclimbed), instead descend
   a few meters to continue the ledge traverse (50 m, 4th,
   low-5th with the error).
9. A long pitch on or near the crest, with some stunning
   exposure. The easiest route may have been to leave the
   crest on the left side. Failing to do that made it harder
   (55m, 5.8).
10. Following the crest in blocks and towers (30 m, 5.0).
11. Amid blocks and towers of the now more-level crest, as
    simul-climbing (60 m, 5.0).
12. Short, simul-climbing pitch to reach a likely route to
    the summit area (20 m, 3rd class).
13. Strenuous chimney and mantel moves to gain the crest.
    Harder than it looked! (15 m, 5.9+). On follow, I hung
    several times, and basically relied on Arjun holding
    the rope to get up!
14. Simul-climbing to the base of the summit block (20 m, 5.0).
15. The climb up and down the summit block, no protection other
    than the belay (8 m, 5.6).

Throughout the climb we enjoyed the scenery and the climbing.
I'd forgotten how exacting granite is, with these big, smooth
shards of rock, where you need an edge or crack to make
progress. I'd forgotten the entertaining way you move up
a mix of cracks and edges, sometimes using counterpressure
to overcome a lack of features.

We'd hoped to climb faster, but had a fair number of
difficulties. It was easy to go wrong in routefinding, and
getting back on track meant downclimbing, or doing some
odd traverse, or climbing through difficult sections.

However, we were happy with the climb! There was one
difficulty that could have made for a miserable hike out:
Arjun's shoes fell off his harness on the difficult pitch
to gain the summit ridge! They fell one at a time as I
watched, horrified, but hoping I could retrieve them.

One shoe wasn't too difficult to get. But the other fell into
a chimney slot which I could just squeeze into, but not
reach my hands down to my feet, where the shoe teetered on
blocks, ready to fall further into caverns I couldn't reach.
I couldn't even bend enough to get my head down more than
a foot.

I had only one choice: pinch my feet together around the shoe,
then lift up my legs and try to mantel out of the chimney
without dropping the shoe. It took a few tries, but this
eventually worked. Thank God! I couldn't imagine walking out
to the car in rock shoes...

We made a rappel, and naturally enough, the rope got stuck.
While I climbed up to retrieve it, Arjun kindly scrambled
up a gully to get our pack and shoes, which we'd left
on the ledges where the shoes were almost lost. We'd mistakenly
thought the descent went by that point.

Now with ordinary shoes, we could climb down the enormous
talus fields to a pass. We'd last seen Scott and Dwayne right
on the summit, enjoying the bouldering moves. We then climbed
down more talus and a snowfield to reach our stashed pack.
We continued down to the lake, and darkness found us on
the slope above Gem Lake.

The slog out felt like it took forever...Arjun calculated that
we were walking very slowly, being completely worn out.
It's funny, I felt like I was walking fast, with the exception
of the far too frequent uphill sections, where I
practically crawled, groaning all the while!

We reached the car at 11, making for a 18 hour day. This was
therefore a bit of an epic. However, I couldn't find a
better partner for such an occasion as Arjun, who was always
happy and cheering us on. A fantastic trip, big thanks to
Arjun!

Oh, thanks to the weather, too! The day before featured
thunderstorms and rain, and there was a chance of it again
after 11 am. But none of that happened.
